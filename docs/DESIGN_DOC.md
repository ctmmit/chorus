# Design: Chorus — Agent-Native Podcast Curation & Voice

## Problem Statement

A knowledge worker follows more podcasts than they can consume (10+ shows
weekly, listening to none in full). The two bad options are spending 8-10
hours a week listening, or relying on generic AI summaries that flatten
every competing take into bland consensus and lose the one segment that
bears on what the principal is actually working on.

Chorus inverts the customer: the user is an **agent, not a human**. An
autonomous runtime (OpenClaw, Hermes, or any agent that reads a SKILL.md
and makes HTTP calls) calls a hosted service that ingests a week of
podcast episodes and hands back two things: a persona-conditioned,
source-cited highlights digest, and a short audio episode where the agent
voices its own opinionated take. Not a summary. A thought partner with a
point of view and a voice.

Forcing function: NandaHack (HCLTech / MIT Media Lab), in-person finale
July 11, 2026. Judged on whether a *different* agent succeeds using only
the SKILL.md, with no human help.

## What Makes This Cool

The agent comes alive. It stops being a tool that summarizes for you and
becomes a colleague that actually listened. Three moves create that:

1. **It has taste.** Curation is conditioned on a soul (the agent's
   analytical lens) plus the principal's live context, so the four
   highlights it surfaces are unmistakably tuned to *this* principal, not
   a generic "top moments" list.
2. **It has a voice.** The agent authors an opinionated script (takes a
   position, pushes back on a guest, links two episodes the principal
   would never have connected) and that script is voiced as a real audio
   episode. The audio is co-equal with the digest, not an encore. It is
   what brings the agent to life.
3. **It can develop its own taste.** Via a short interview, the agent
   refines its own soul and curates independently from the principal —
   reporting back not "here's what you asked for" but "here's what *I*
   found interesting."

## Premises (after challenge + user revision)

1. **Audio is co-equal core, not an encore.** Digest and opinionated
   episode are both the deliverable; audio is what makes the agent feel
   alive. Resilience still applies: one episode failing to render must not
   sink the run.
2. **The real demo risk is curation taste, not plumbing.** `path_test.sh`
   asserts structure, citation-resolution, and refusal — never "good
   highlights." Fixtures must make the persona lens *visible* (same
   episodes, swap the soul, watch highlights change) or the creativity
   signal is invisible.
3. **Server-side transcript sourcing is solvable but not free; defer the
   how to eng research.** Candidate: audio pull (RSS enclosure or `yt-dlp`)
   + server-side Whisper transcription. Caveat I over-claimed initially:
   pulling *audio* from YouTube datacenter IPs can still be throttled or
   403'd, and Whisper over a week of 5-6 full episodes is real compute
   (minutes-to-tens-of-minutes, GPU-sensitive) that collides with the audio
   latency bound. The clean mitigation is **pre-transcribed fixtures** so
   `path_test.sh` never depends on live transcription. Resolved in
   `/plan-eng-review`, not a blocker.
4. **The audio engine is a genuine open fork.** NotebookLM (great banter,
   but it writes its own script — agent loses authorship of its takes) vs
   ElevenLabs (agent keeps authorship, you own the quality problem) vs an
   open-source podcast-gen framework (your LLM writes the opinionated
   two-host script, a TTS engine voices it — authorship *and* two-host
   format, quality unverified). Decided after research in eng planning.
5. **Build the real thing; the demo is just the venue.** Sync-vs-async is
   a pure UX call (audio takes minutes, so it is async — that is reality),
   not a demo hedge. A demo can be pre-loaded if needed.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Voice-first wedge (minimal viable)
Caller passes soul + context + episode list (original §6). All energy into
audio quality and visibly persona-tuned curation. Presets, charts, and the
agent-interview deferred. Effort: M. Risk: Low-Med. Coverage vs full
vision: 6/10. Ships the alive-agent core for certain; leaves the most
exciting idea on the table.

### Approach B: Full taste platform (ideal / expansive) — SELECTED
A, plus taste presets, charts discovery (top 10, business, tech, pop
culture), plus the agent-interview onboarding that builds/refines the soul.
Effort: XL. Risk: High if built big-bang. Coverage: 10/10. Closest to the
full vision; mitigated by the layered structure below.

### Approach C: Agent-as-curator (creative / lateral)
A's voice-first core plus only the agent-interview expansion (the agent
develops its own taste and curates independently). Skip charts/presets.
Effort: M-L. Risk: Med. Coverage: 8/10. The most on-thesis novelty without
the discovery-surface weight.

## Recommended Approach

**B is the destination; the committed v1 is the spine plus the
agent-interview layer; charts/presets are post-July-11 stretch.** The user
chose B for ambition, with full knowledge of the risk. Honesty requires
naming the realistic floor: a solo builder in three weeks ships the spine
plus at most one layer. So the design commits to **spine + agent-interview**
(the thesis layer) as the real deliverable, treats pick-and-choose as a
fast follow, and explicitly defers charts/presets past the finale. Calling
the target "B" without this floor would set a false expectation; the
committed shape is closer to Approach C, with B's discovery surface as a
sequenced roadmap.

This reconciles the ambition with the vibe-code-framework's "one golden
path per version" discipline: **the spine is the v1 golden path; layers are
sequenced ROADMAP goals that only begin once the spine is green, ordered
most-thesis-first so the differentiator is never the thing sacrificed.** If
time runs out, the spine plus whatever layers are green ships.

**Spine (v1 golden path — must go green first):**
1. Agent provides a soul (passed in for the spine; refined via interview in
   Layer 1).
2. Caller passes context + episode selection.
3. Soul-conditioned curation → cited digest. Every highlight resolves to a
   real transcript timestamp; episodes whose top segment fails to clear the
   relevance threshold return explicit "nothing cleared the bar" rather than
   an invented reason (this is the "refusal" the path_test asserts — the
   trigger is a per-episode relevance score below a set bar, not a free-text
   query).
4. Agent authors an opinionated script; script is voiced → audio episode.
   **Minimum acceptable audio (spine floor): a single-voice opinionated
   monologue.** Two-host dialogue is a layer, so the thesis never hinges on
   an unproven dialogue-TTS path.
5. Service returns `{digest, script, audio_url}` (digest synchronous and
   fast; audio async behind one poll endpoint with `pending|done|failed`).
6. `scripts/path_test.sh` green on **5 fixture sets across 3 categories**:
   3 clean, 1 malformed / missing-transcript, 1 ungrounded.
7. **SKILL.md is dogfooded by a second, fresh agent against the live
   endpoint** — a cold agent completes the golden path with no human help.
   This is a spine exit criterion, not an aspiration.

**Provisional interface (firm up in eng review):**
- Request: `POST /digest { soul: string (markdown), context: string,
  episodes: [{ url | rss_guid | show+date }], highlight_count?: int }`
- Response: `{ job_id }` → `GET /digest/{job_id}` →
  `{ status: pending|done|failed, digest: [{ episode, segment_timestamp,
  relevance_score, one_line_reason }], script, audio_url }`
- **Soul artifact:** free-form `soul.md` (identity, attention triggers,
  communication style) injected into the curation prompt. One real example
  checked into `/fixtures`.

**Layer 1 — Agent-interview curation (the thesis layer, build first).** The
agent is interviewed, builds / refines its soul, and curates independently
from the principal. Exit: two distinct souls over the same episodes produce
demonstrably different digests (this also doubles as the Premise-2
visibility fixture and the demo's single most convincing moment).

**Layer 2 — Episode/show pick-and-choose.** Caller selects specific
episodes or shows rather than passing a raw list. Exit: path_test through
the selection endpoint green.

**Layer 3 — Taste presets + charts (post-July-11 stretch).** Quick-picks:
top 10 on the charts, business, tech, pop culture. Adds a podcast-charts
data source — the least on-thesis layer carrying the most external risk
(licensing + server-side access, Open Q4). Deferred past the finale unless
the spine and Layers 1-2 land with time to spare.

## Open Questions (resolve in /plan-eng-review)

1. **Audio engine choice.** Research NotebookLM API status (2026) and
   steering/customization, ElevenLabs V3 Text-to-Dialogue availability and
   the agent-authored-script path, and open-source podcastfy-style
   frameworks. Score on: authorship preservation, audio quality, latency,
   cost, setup difficulty. This is the single load-bearing technical
   decision.
2. **Server-side transcript sourcing.** Distinguish RSS-audio pull from
   YouTube pull (different failure modes). Validate `yt-dlp`/RSS + Whisper
   from the actual target host vs a paid transcript API. Benchmark Whisper
   wall-clock and cost per run on the hobby tier; confirm audio download is
   not IP-blocked. Plan for pre-transcribed fixtures.
3. **Latency bounds.** Set the digest `[N]s` and audio-job `[M]min` numbers
   once the engine is chosen. These become `path_test.sh` assertions.
4. **Charts data source (Layer 2).** Which podcast-charts source (Apple /
   Spotify / Podcast Index), license, and does it work server-side.
5. **Soul-version provenance.** Curation output records which soul version
   produced it (matters once Layer 3 lets the soul change).

## Success Criteria

- Spine: `scripts/path_test.sh` green on 5 fixture sets (3 clean, 1
  malformed, 1 ungrounded) — citations resolve to real timestamps,
  below-threshold episodes refuse, latency within the bounds set in Open
  Q3. Citation-resolution check: the cited timestamp ± a window contains the
  quoted span (fuzzy match against the transcript).
- A cold agent completes the golden path from SKILL.md alone, no human help.
- The persona lens is visibly demonstrable: same episodes, two souls, two
  different digests.
- A produced audio episode that takes a position and is traceable to
  surfaced highlights.
- Each layer green is independently checkable and additive over the spine.
- Kill criterion (resource-denominated, per IDEA_DOC §12): set the $[X] /
  [N]-hours bound in the engineering pass.

## The Assignment

Before any code: assemble the **two-soul visibility fixture** by hand. Take
one real week of 5-6 episodes, write two genuinely different `soul.md`
files (e.g., your investor lens vs a pop-culture-critic lens), and hand-run
the curation prompt for both. If the two highlight sets don't come out
obviously different, the core thesis isn't real yet and no amount of
plumbing fixes it. If they do, you've just built your demo's single most
convincing moment and your Layer-3 exit criterion in one afternoon.
